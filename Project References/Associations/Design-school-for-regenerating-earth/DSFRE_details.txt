design-school-for-regenerating-earth.mn.co is the online community hub (hosted on the Mighty Networks platform) for the Design School for Regenerating Earth (DS4RE). Co-founded by Joe Brewer and Penny Heiple, this initiative is a global community of practice designed to operationalize the vision laid out in Brewer’s book, The Design Pathway for Regenerating Earth.

Its primary mission is to help humanity evolve into a "planetary species" by organizing local action around bioregions—holistic, integrated landscapes—and connecting these regions into a planetary network of learning and exchange 
2 
6.

Core Philosophy: The Bioregional Pathway
The school is built on the premise that to regenerate the Earth at the necessary scale, humanity must move beyond fragmented efforts and organize around bioregional economies 
1.

The Goal: To weave individual landscapes into cohesive bioregions and restore harmony to entire ecosystems through coordinated, place-based action 
1.
The Method: It combines local, on-the-ground regeneration with a global "planetary network of learning exchanges," allowing communities to share tools, languages, and strategies across continents 
2 
6.
Evolutionary Shift: The school views this work as a conscious evolution of human cooperation, requiring unprecedented levels of collaboration to realign humanity with living systems 
6.
Structure and Activities
The Design School functions as both an educational institution and a collaborative network.

Learning Formats: Members participate in webinars, exploratory sessions, learning journeys, design labs, and on-the-ground community engagements. Topics range from earth systems and regenerative design to trauma healing and prosocial processes 
1.
Place-Based Hubs: A key feature is the Place-Based Hub system. There are currently at least twelve Bioregional Resource Hubs active globally, organizing at scales from watersheds to sub-continental bioregions (e.g., Cascadia, the Driftless Area) 
3 
5.
Member-Led Spaces: Engaged members can host their own activities, such as guilds, workgroups, themed dialogues, and courses, fostering a bottom-up, co-creative culture 
2.
Cohorts: The school runs specific regional cohorts, such as the Cascadian Cohort, which began in early 2026 to deepen bioregional understanding and action in the Pacific Northwest 
5.
Membership and Access
The school operates on a subscription model to support its ongoing activities and resources.

Cost: Members can join for $50 per month or $500 per year, granting full access to learning resources, the community platform, and ongoing activities
