redcasalatina.org is the digital hub for Red CASA Latina (Network of CASA Latina), a regional network dedicated to connecting and strengthening sustainable settlements, eco-villages, and regenerative communities across Latin America.

It is important to distinguish this entity from CASA Latina (the worker center in Seattle, USA), which shares a similar name but has a completely different mission focused on immigrant labor rights in the United States. While the Seattle organization is a "worker center," Red CASA Latina is a network of networks focused on ecological sustainability, "good living" (buen vivir), and territorial regeneration in Latin America 
6.

Core Mission and Philosophy
The network operates on the principle of Pluriculturalism and Harmony with Mother Earth. Its goal is to articulate a network of organizations and initiatives that promote sustainable settlements and regenerative lifestyles.

Purpose: To facilitate the connection between sustainable settlements in Latin America, allowing for the exchange of information, knowledge, experiences, and services 
6.
Vision: To strengthen national and regional networks, supporting local initiatives and achieving "good living" (buen vivir) in the territories where these settlements operate 
6.
Values: The network emphasizes living in fullness, peace, and harmony with the Earth and all beings, promoting styles of life that are regenerative rather than extractive 
6.
Key Activities and Structure
Red CASA Latina functions as an articulator and connector rather than a single physical organization.

Networking: It links various eco-villages, sustainable communities, and related initiatives across Latin America, creating a "network of networks" 
6.
Education and Research: The network runs the C-Ecosistémica project, a circle of education and research that hosts webinars and discussions on "ecosocial regeneration" and features key figures in the Latin American regenerative movement 
6.
Events and Training: They organize gatherings and training sessions, such as "Art of Hosting" workshops, often in collaboration with local partners like Casa Alvernia in Chile .
Advocacy: The network advocates for the rights of "Mother Earth" (Derechos de la Madre Tierra) and promotes the creation of sustainable settlements and alliances 
6.
