# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

- People exploring biodiversity work, ecovillages, communities, volunteering, and learning opportunities in the Yucatan Peninsula.
- Community organizers and project stewards who need a clear public record of initiatives, evidence, updates, events, and ways to participate.
- Funders, practitioners, and partners learning how biodiversity projects may progress from local stewardship toward credible standards, accreditation, and regenerative finance.

## Product Purpose

Sol Kin Ecovillage is a public bilingual-ready information and reporting website for a growing biodiversity and community network in the Yucatan Peninsula. It must make the network understandable in the first viewport, document current and developing initiatives honestly, help visitors find ways to join or volunteer, explain biodiversity economy and accreditation pathways, preserve the cooperative history supplied for Ejido X-Hazil Sur, and support future publication of projects, communities, organizations, events, progress evidence, and participation opportunities.

English is required. Source Spanish should remain visible where it carries original project history. A complete Spanish edition is not yet established and must not be implied.

## Positioning

The product combines an accessible regional field atlas with an evidence ledger: visitors can discover initiatives and communities while seeing what is documented, what is developing, and which external standards or financing frameworks are being studied. It must distinguish project truth from aspirations instead of presenting unverified impact claims.

## Operating Context

- Static deployment on standard Hostinger hosting, with no paid or unavailable backend dependency.
- Non-developers should be able to add structured local content for projects, communities, updates, events, organizations, progress evidence, and calls to action.
- Funding and support surfaces must be honest interest/contact pathways unless a real payment mechanism is configured later.
- Primary reference materials are stored under `Photos/` and `Project References/`; originals must be preserved.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Required surfaces: home, projects, project detail/reporting, community discovery, biodiversity economy learning, accreditation/standards guidance, cooperative story, organizations, events/updates, and support/volunteer pathways.
- Current initiative names confirmed by the project brief: Mayan Learning Center, Mayan Bee Sanctuary, and an eco-touristic retreat connected to the supplied Ejido X-Hazil Sur cooperative material. Their operational details, locations, contacts, dates, and outcomes are not established by repository evidence and must be marked as developing or pending confirmation.
- BFF is confirmed in repository material as **Bioregional Financing Facility**: a community-owned institution using participatory, transparent, place-based financial governance to support regenerative transition.
- The Global Biodiversity Standard (TGBS) is a relevant external restoration-quality framework. It does not itself issue biodiversity credits, and no repository evidence shows that a Sol Kin or X-Hazil initiative is certified, accredited, or issuing credits.
- The brief mentions roughly nine local ecovillages or communities, but the repository provides no verified roster. The discovery experience must therefore support future entries without inventing names.
- Association reference files establish organization names and public descriptions but do not establish endorsement, partnership, or permission to use logos publicly. Any relationship status remains an open decision.
- Public forms must work within static-hosting constraints, validate accessibly, and never claim a successful submission without a configured endpoint.
- Canonical production URL, public contact address, form endpoint, confirmed community roster, organization relationship status, and complete Spanish translation are open decisions.

## Brand Commitments

- Product name: **Sol Kin Ecovillage**.
- Existing identity assets: three square Sol Kin logo variants in `Photos/brand/`, using a bold stacked wordmark, a small tree mark, black/white, and a vivid green ground.
- Voice: factual, invitational, regionally grounded, respectful of Maya and Yucatan contexts, and explicit about evidence status.
- Avoid stereotypical pseudo-Maya decoration, invented Indigenous claims, fabricated impact metrics, generic eco-site tropes, and unsupported partner language.

## Evidence on Hand

- `Project References/project_support_materials/historia-ejido-x-hazil-sur_compress.txt`: Spanish history of Ejido X-Hazil y Anexos in Quintana Roo. It documents a 1936 founding, 398 ejidatarios across Chancah Veracruz, X-Hazil, and Uh May, a 55,020-hectare territory bordering the Sian Ka'an Biosphere Reserve, forestry and chicle practices, a 1946 twenty-hive apiary organized by six beekeepers, a 1,434-hectare community forest reserve established in 2003, and forest-management certificate SW-FM/COC-1517 obtained in May 2005. The document does not establish a current tourism retreat, a named bee sanctuary, or a Mayan Learning Center.
- `Project References/project_support_materials/TGBS-Manual-English-Med-res.txt`: 2024 Global Biodiversity Standard manual published by Botanic Gardens Conservation International and the Society for Ecological Restoration. It documents eight assessment criteria, pre-certification, Standard/Advanced/Premium tiers, and five-year certification validity.
- `Project References/project_support_materials/Bioregional-Financing-Facilities-ebook-2024.txt`: June 2024 BioFi Project publication defining BFFs and describing community-owned, place-based financing structures and possible eco-credit program roles.
- `Project References/project_support_materials/The_Design_Pathway_for_Regenerating_Earth.txt`: Joe Brewer's general regenerative design reference. It does not connect its methods to Sol Kin or X-Hazil.
- `Project References/Associations/`: secondary research notes and logos for the BioFi Project, Design School for Regenerating Earth, EcoHubs Community, The Holomovement, and Red CASA Latina. These files do not prove formal association or endorsement.
- `Photos/brand/`: three authentic Sol Kin logo variants. No authentic landscape, people, species, project, or event photography is currently present.
- No verified local ecovillage roster, testimonials, public contact channel, event schedule, active funding mechanism, project result data, or current accreditation record is present.

## Product Principles

1. Show evidence status as clearly as project status.
2. Make regional participation easier without manufacturing certainty.
3. Keep living content structured, local, and maintainable.
4. Teach standards and finance as pathways, not promises.
5. Let source material and field reporting lead the story.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG-minded accessibility across semantic structure, keyboard navigation, visible focus, color contrast, reduced motion, responsive layouts, non-hover access, accessible validation, and useful source-based image descriptions. English is the complete launch language; Spanish source material is preserved without presenting the site as fully translated.
